# Arts & Culture — 1778

Cultural landmarks, works, and institutions of 1778.

The year 1778 witnessed a paradoxical moment in European cultural history: the simultaneous deaths of two towering Enlightenment figures, Voltaire and Rousseau, and the opening of one of Europe's greatest opera houses, La Scala in Milan. Voltaire's final return to Paris in February was celebrated as a triumphant recognition of his lifetime of wit, satire, and intellectual combat against religious fanaticism and dogma. Rousseau's death in July marked the closure of another philosophical voice that had shaped the Enlightenment's turn toward emotion, nature, and democratic theory. And yet, even as these intellectual giants departed the stage, the inauguration of La Scala on August 3, 1778 with Salieri's opera demonstrated that European culture was not diminishing but transforming—institutional, public, and operatic splendor would carry forward the artistic legacy that the philosophes had championed and critiqued in equal measure. 1778 thus embodied both an ending and a beginning in European artistic and intellectual life.
