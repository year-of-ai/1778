# Political Events — 1778

Alliances, declarations of war, and governance changes of 1778.

The political landscape of 1778 pivoted on the formal recognition of American independence by France and the resulting global expansion of the Revolutionary War. The February alliance between France and the United States transformed an internal British colonial rebellion into a world conflict, drawing European powers into competition for global influence. Britain's March declaration of war on France widened the conflict across the Atlantic, the Caribbean, and beyond. Meanwhile, Britain's desperate Carlisle Peace Commission attempted reconciliation with the colonies through political concessions, but Congress's rejection of the offer hardened American war aims toward complete independence, making diplomatic resolution impossible. In parallel, ten of the thirteen states ratified the Articles of Confederation during 1778, giving the union a constitutional framework that reinforced its credibility as a French ally. These events collectively reshaped the geopolitical order and ensured that American independence would be won on a world stage rather than settled through negotiation.
